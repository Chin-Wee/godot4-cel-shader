# Animation direction for the 9 m, 10 t mech

**Flight articulation:** [Pose-driven aerial mech articulation](demo/aerial/FLIGHT_ARTICULATION_DESIGN.md) specifies the fixed-axis laboratory and the aerial demo’s authored upper-body layers and stress-driven corrective gestures.

**This is the source of truth for mech movement architecture, animation, and combat animation design.** The [ground demo README](demo/ground/README.md) and [aerial demo README](demo/aerial/README.md) describe current controls, implementation and verification. The [Unreal integration](#unreal-engine-58-integration) is a proposed playable prototype. Use source and replay results to verify what the demos currently do.

The mech should feel heavy because the player can see **where it takes load, what accelerates it, and how it arrests the motion**. A Gustav Karl-style run can be fast. The loaded boot stays readable, the hips drive over it, and the next step lands with purpose. In flight, a jet can react quickly while the velocity vector takes time to change. In combat, a weapon can move quickly through its strike while the chassis takes longer to recover.

The timing ranges below are **first-pass authoring targets** for our chassis. The film's Gustav Karl reference rig is for a 22–23 m mobile suit. I reviewed the production boot diagram and walk reel, and used the film staff's breakdowns for the action sequences. The references establish movement and staging choices. The proposed timings need to be judged in our demo. [Autodesk production breakdown](https://area.autodesk.jp/case/animation/hathaway-the-witch-of-circe/) · [Gustav Karl walk reel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=No6J_w2lkdE) · [Film staff on the Uluru action](https://cgworld.jp/article/202604-hathaway.html)

## What makes motion feel heavy

| Cue | What to author | How to judge it |
|---|---|---|
| **Load** | Move the pelvis toward the support leg, lower it through the knee and ankle, and hold the loaded contact through the change in momentum. In air, bring the legs, chest or shoulders into a posture that visibly prepares the applicable nozzles or armor surfaces. | Pause at the start of the move. The viewer should be able to point to the boot or thrust system doing the work. |
| **Spacing** | Put frames close together as the mech gathers. Increase spacing through the push or strike. Compress at contact, then ease out over more frames. Key the extremes and passing pose by hand. | At full speed, identify preparation, decisive action and recovery in one silhouette. Stepping through frames should reveal a fast action between clearly different poses. |
| **Joint sequence** | Key pelvis, chest and weapon first. Key feet, shoulders and weapon grip against their constraints. Let an attached plate or free arm finish a little later where the joint permits it. | A loaded knee and a loose forearm react differently. There should be one controlled settle, with persistent motion only where forces continue. |
| **Ground reference** | Track the active heel, sole or toe contact in world space. Allow slip only when the contact physically slides. Move the pelvis and knee to absorb the load without moving the planted contact point. | Watch from the side with the floor visible. Any drift in the loaded point will read even if the hips and shoulders look good. |
| **Scale and consequence** | Use a stable street-level camera, a full silhouette, and nearby doors, lamps and vehicles. Time the footfall, dust and shadow to contact. Drive skid dust by measured slip and jet light by delivered thrust. | Watch once without effects and once with them. The first pass should still show weight. The second should make the cause and the scale clearer. |

Timing and spacing are classical tools for communicating mass. For this project, combine them with the film team's grounded camera layout and the way ILM described staging speed and mechanical articulation in *Pacific Rim*. The Jaegers are a reference for readable loads and follow-through. Our travel speed and stance cadence come from our own rig. [John Lasseter's SIGGRAPH paper](https://www.cs.utexas.edu/~fussell/courses/cs384g-fall2011/projects/animator/lasseter.pdf) · [Hathaway production interview](https://area.autodesk.jp/case/animation/hathaway-the-witch-of-circe/) · [Pacific Rim director and animation supervisor interview](https://www.awn.com/vfxworld/pacific-rim-and-vfx-robot-porn)

### Put numbers against the silhouette

- **Ground redirect.** At 10 t, rotating a 4 m/s velocity by 90° at the same speed requires about **56.6 kN·s** of net horizontal impulse. With 0.70 grip and full weight on level ground, the existing load model gives at most **68.7 kN** tangentially. Even ideal constant grip needs about **0.82 s** of support to do that with feet alone. Load transfer, partial support and the actual controller add time. If upward thrust carries half the weight, the foot force budget halves. Author the late turn so an available step brakes, then the next legal plant redirects. A fast hip turn at the request frame would hide the missing impulse. [Ground traction implementation](demo/ground/ground_motion.gd) · [Impulse and momentum](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/9-2-impulse-and-collisions)
- **Landing.** A 5 m/s normal impact at 10 t carries **50 kN·s** of normal momentum and **125 kJ** of kinetic energy in that component. Use precontact normal speed and the actual contact as the inputs for a soft or hard catch. Use tangential speed independently for skid. Knee and hip compression are authored and corrected by IK. The capsule still resolves impact. The numbers describe the body at contact and do not assign loads to individual leg joints. [DeVita and Skelly landing experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1548984/) · [Existing contact pose](demo/aerial/contact_pose.gd)
- **Flight.** Main thrust, cold gas and aero must deliver the actual changes in linear and angular momentum. A bent calf moves the outlet and changes its torque arm. Test a new pose under full throttle and check aggregate force, residual moment, gimbal travel and opposing cold ports before approving its silhouette. Near zero speed, a gimballed jet can still steer while aerodynamic leverage drops. In a coast, an attitude change can leave the direction of travel almost unchanged. [Nozzle and surface geometry](demo/aerial/aerial_mech.gd) · [NASA small spacecraft propulsion](https://www.nasa.gov/smallsat-institute/sst-soa/in-space_propulsion/)

For ranged combat, author **carry, aim and brace** as reusable poses, plus weapon-specific fire and reload actions. A rifle stays on target with a small upper-body correction. Once the target moves outside the shoulder's aim envelope, the body has to reorient through a step or yaw demand. For a shoulder cannon, key the mount clearing the armor, then the chest settling behind the line of fire. A crouched cover peek should show where the muzzle clears the wall before the blast lights the scene. In a melee grapple, key the two bodies against a common grip point. A grab can branch to a paired move only after reach and contact checks agree. A shove or glancing contact can exit into each character's own recovery. ## Movement architecture and constraints

One rigid body carries position and momentum through ground and air. Native collision and gravity provide contact and weight. Ground traction, hot and cold nozzles, and posed aerodynamic surfaces supply the commanded forces and moments. The same physical solver delivers every burst, hop, and turn. Animation can change the geometry feeding that solver through articulated joints. It cannot set chassis velocity, rotation, or displacement. [Body integration](demo/aerial/aerial_mech.gd) · [Shared force path](demo/aerial/flight_controller.gd)

### Ground locomotion

- Use authored joint poses and swing arcs for steady gait. Match the phase to measured travel within a bounded rate. Starts, stops, changes of stance, and committed cuts have their own contact markers. Fit only the next reachable contacts during a transition. Keep the loaded heel or toe in terrain space and preserve its heading through support. Adjust a free swing before commitment. After commitment, retain the plant and hold the latest requested direction for the next legal step.
- A right cut prefers a left outside plant when reach and support permit it. Rank reachable contacts against the predicted center of mass and momentum on the support plane. A simple starting estimate offsets predicted position by `v / sqrt(g / h)`, where `h` is effective center-of-mass height and `v` is planar approach velocity. Shorten or add a step if the original target fails reach, clearance, or wall checks. At 4 m/s, the first-pass target is three to four contacts in about 2 s, with exit travel and facing within 15° of the requested direction. [Hof's balance model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17935808/) · [Uncharted 4 traversal actions](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/uncharted_4_a_thiefs_end_the_5_year_anniversary_retrospective)
- Validate a boot's reachable floor sample against current native support before applying traction. The capsule and shallow ground pads provide normal reaction. Allocate the projected body weight less delivered upward force across the loaded feet. Cap their tangential forces at 0.70 of load for grip and 0.45 for skid. Double support allows an opposed foot couple for yaw. The loss of support removes traction immediately. [Ground force path](demo/ground/ground_motion.gd)
- Keep the 90° standing pivot separate from the moving cut. It uses two placements and settles within 5° of target. A supported hop captures direction on the input edge, defaults forward, and requests 0.5 s of hot thrust. Start the 1 s cooldown when the request begins. Holding the key, loss of support, or cooldown prevents a repeat. From rest, target 0.8–1.6 m rise and 3–6 m to touchdown. Allow one short cushion when predicted contact and valve readiness agree. A normal speed of at least 5 m/s requires 0.45 s recovery before another hop.

### Flight

- W pitches down and S pulls up. A/D rolls and Q/E yaws in both plane and space flight, including bank and inversion. Powered, aligned flight has soft ±30° airflow pitch protection. Shift permits intentional departure and opens the relevant armor surfaces. Normal steering uses compact weight transfer and shallow aerodynamic corrections. Shift braking follows the actual completed surface projection as the pose opens and closes. Cutoff, detached flow, or an underpowered climb may preserve substantial travel while available acceleration changes.
- A powered hover direction tap starts a 0.5 s burst request with its captured direction and a 1 s activation-to-activation cooldown. It can follow another accepted tap when the cooldown expires. Holding the key and early taps produce no repeat. The cruise detent stays independent, and engine cutoff fades delivered force. Allocate the back, calf, heel and cold nozzles together. Preserve force direction and attitude reserve with per-port caps, gimbal limits, and finite ignition, minimum burn, shutdown and restart. Feasible delivery determines magnitude. [Flight controller and limits](demo/aerial/flight_controller.gd)
- Try hot vectoring before cold assistance. Cold jets coast through small errors and pulse for acquisition, correction or arrest. Minimize fuel use and avoid cancelling jets except when their force couple supplies a needed moment. Their limits rise linearly from 15% of rating at 0 m/s to 100% at 40 m/s. Engine-off flight keeps actual momentum, and the remaining cold jets and aero govern attitude response.
- Keep full-body poses continuous between hover, fighter flight, coast and fall. Use effective body-relative rates and actual revolutions for flip and roll poses. Engine cutoff or Shift allows a full forward or back flip within the current 1.95 rad/s request ceiling. A blocked maneuver holds its physical phase. Release and reversal can interrupt at any angle. Preserve servo velocities and favor launch or landing over the acrobatic pose when they overlap. Neck look is bounded and independent of the panels and nozzles. The existing rigid body and capsule define inertia. [Flight pose and maneuver phases](demo/aerial/flight_pose.gd)

## Author the beats on the actual chassis

1. **Block a shot and its gameplay conditions.** Use the actual mech skeleton and armor. Record input, trajectory, entry speed, contact side and the action that can follow. Put markers at the load-bearing events. Use a side camera for contact and a three-quarter view for silhouette. Observe how the body moves over the active foot in the reference before keying toes or armor details.
2. **Key the physical chain first.** For ground work, place the support foot and establish its heel, sole and toe pivots. Key pelvis, trunk and swing ankle around it. For flight, start with the existing hover, stream, brake and acrobatic pose keys. Key the chest and knees in response to available thrust and airflow. For weapon work, block the weapon or blade path, then key the supporting hand and shoulder. Check skirt, calf, heel and backpack clearance across the full swept movement.
3. **Block stepped, then polish splines.** Review blocking at **30 fps** so load and reversal frames stand out. Use tangent handles and breakdown poses for a quicker transfer through drive and a longer controlled return. Make the hinge axes and armor limits legible. Mirror only with explicit left/right pairing, and give a shield or rifle side its own shoulders and grips.
4. **Bake editable actions to the existing rig.** Author in Blender or with the existing Godot pose studies. Preserve skeleton names and rest poses. Bake a reference root track for stride, heading and timing, and joint-local tracks for playback. Export FBX for Unreal and glTF for Godot from the same authored actions. Inspect one round trip before exporting a whole library, including a planted toe, a bent calf nozzle and a support-hand grip. [Blender Actions](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.5/animation/actions.html) · [Blender glTF export](https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/4.2/addons/import_export/scene_gltf2.html) · [Godot AnimationLibrary import](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/assets_pipeline/importing_3d_scenes/import_configuration.html)
5. **Fit the pose to live conditions.** Use a small selection table keyed by support, gait or flight phase, intent, current direction and weapon stance. Enter a compatible clip phase, preserve the blend velocity and let the existing IK make bounded corrections. A foot that cannot reach gives the player a gather step, a skid or a recovery. The planted foot and blade arc take precedence over torso ornament. [Epic's 5.8 Chooser and Blend Stack](https://www.unrealengine.com/tech-blog/download-the-latest-game-animation-sample-project-now-updated-for-ue-5-8) · [Overgrowth animation talk](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020583/Animation-Bootcamp-An-Indie-Approach)

### Ownership and tick order

The proposed names describe a prototype. Start with one Pawn, one movement component, one AnimBP and one contact rig. Keep ground, boost, cruise and landing in the same movement component and the same Chaos body.

1. **Input and simulation.** Use Enhanced Input for axes and edge-triggered actions. Queue tap edges until consumed by a physics tick, even when several ticks run in one rendered frame. Start the single 10,000 kg body at a fixed **60 Hz**. Port the Godot per-port allocator, valve and gimbal timing, panel force model and support budget as plain simulation code. The capsule owns normal reaction with a zero-friction combine setup verified against the street material. Add bounded foot traction only while a reachable anchor agrees with a current supporting body contact. Apply traction, nozzle and aerodynamic point forces once at the physics boundary. Body transform and momentum come from Chaos.
2. **Fixed-tick geometry.** Bake the joints affecting a nozzle, panel or force-bearing foot onto a fixed-time track or reuse the existing servo targets. Use the same skeleton calibration for that track and the visual mesh. At tick N the solver uses the latest completed mechanical pose, normally N−1, to transform hardpoints and panel normals into the current body's frame. Publish the completed body state and the next mechanical pose from tick N. Use solver contact for support and touchdown so traction shuts off as soon as support is absent. An async tick takes plain input and calibrated geometry and uses the physics-thread body and contact interfaces. Scene queries prepared on the game thread can propose future floor targets. Track their age and gate traction on solver contact.
3. **Animation handoff.** Copy the latest complete `FMechSimSnapshot` into the AnimInstance on the game thread before its parallel animation update. Use Property Access or an AnimInstance proxy copy for worker-thread reads. Include the tick number, signed body-local velocity and flow, accepted action and phase, both anchor transforms, and precontact and delivered-force measurements. Interpolate completed poses against a consistent body time for rendering. Use the same visual body sample for the chase camera. Mechanical geometry updates on the fixed clock before it is published to the AnimBP. This keeps evaluating render sockets from changing the force solve.
4. **Prove calibration at the API boundary.** UE uses +X forward, +Y right and +Z up. Map the source frame and check thrust and moment signs at each labeled mount. At the native centimeter-based physics boundary, convert m and m/s by ×100, N by ×100 for a kg·cm/s² force API, and N·m by ×10,000 where torque is applied directly. Keep the allocator in SI. Validate with gravity and a point-force test. For a centered horizontal 10 kN push on 10,000 kg, the ideal change is **1 m/s over 1 s** with other horizontal forces disabled. Check an off-center push for the expected sign of angular acceleration. ### Right cut and powered repositioning

**Ground cut.** A right input enters the gather immediately. Let head and free shoulder start the look while the mech still travels forward. Check the next legal plant against reach, approach speed and remaining support time. A left outside plant needs a position from which it can brake and push right. Preserve an already committed landing and take a gather step when that plant cannot do the job. Store the accepted plant point, contact range and target travel heading. Author the pelvis lowering into the loaded knee. The foot supplies the bounded braking and lateral force, the trailing foot gathers into the new lane, and chassis heading follows once redirection is underway. Crouch has its own phase-compatible gait and lowered pelvis. Preserve the loaded contact through crouch entry. Repeat the same cut from both lead phases and with a cue after commit.

**Ground search.** Once the paired entries exist, compare the direct Chooser to `PSS_Mech_Cut` / `PSD_Mech_Cut` and a separate stop schema/database. Start with 60 Hz index samples. Compare recent pose and foot positions and velocities with the continuing animation. For the cut compare achievable trajectory and heading at roughly +0.15, +0.30 and +0.50 s, with recent samples to preserve approach. Project the query onto the support plane. Predict from actual velocity and bounded traction, including the expected braking distance, and track facing separately. For a stop favor foot phase, velocity decay and remaining distance. Eligibility gates the search. Rank only phases with compatible load, lead and commit state. Pose Search first chooses among visual variants within the approved mechanical entry. A later search can propose a new mechanical clip at a decision window. The fixed tick validates it before commit and records the chosen asset and time in the replay. [Motion Matching](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-in-unreal-engine) · [Motion Matching debugger](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/en-us/unreal-engine/motion-matching-debugging-in-unreal-engine)

**Flight response.** Choose an asymmetric starting pose using the accepted boost direction, then drive the visible extension by delivered thrust and the brake pose by the signed turn command plus measured speed. Shift selects the authored inside/outside aero posture on the fixed mechanical track. Check that the opened surfaces give the anticipated aerodynamic moment and speed loss. Use signed local velocity, airflow and body angular velocity to blend cruise, bank, glide, broadside fall and coast. Advance a full roll or flip by measured integrated angular travel. Change to a new recovery pose on release using the current angle and rates. A failed valve opening stays in preparation, fall or recovery as appropriate. Reach at descent when time-to-contact and a valid floor target agree. Contact chooses the catch. Precontact normal speed chooses the load, and tangential slip chooses skid. Keep these first flight clips in a Chooser and blend by measured quantities. When multiple authored entries produce visible pops, test a separate flight schema with 3D trajectory and quaternion-facing samples from our snapshot-based predictor. Keep thrust, airflow and contact eligibility in Chooser data. Bone pose and 3D travel belong in Pose Search channels.
