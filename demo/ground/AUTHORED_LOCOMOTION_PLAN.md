# Ground Mode v2 · Gustav Karl locomotion

The **9 m, 10 t** mech should walk with purpose, run shoulder-first under fire, and crouch into a lower guard. At a sharp input change it should visibly take weight, push off into the new lane, then bring its body around. Judge the motion in the existing chase and pedestrian views with a clear view of both boots.

In the current replay, the knees stay bent, the arms hang wide, and each stride follows the same slow lift and set-down. The source has one 0.70 s swing template and adds ground cues to the flight pose. The existing pivot also turns the chassis before stepping and waits for near-idle to begin. ## Production and biomechanics references

- **Uncharted 4** is the shipped-game architecture reference. Naughty Dog's NPC traversal chooses an authored Traversal Action Pack and decides where it will exit before the current action ends. Use that pattern for a small set of authored locomotion actions with contact-compatible exits. **Hathaway: The Sorcery of Nymph Circe** is the visual reference. Its Gustav Karl Type-00 ankle rig keeps the toe down while the heel lifts and rolls the articulated boot off the pavement. The shoulder run and crouch below are targets for this chassis. [Naughty Dog traversal retrospective](https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/uncharted_4_a_thiefs_end_the_5_year_anniversary_retrospective) · [Gustav Karl ankle rig and walk reel](https://area.autodesk.jp/case/animation/hathaway-the-witch-of-circe/)
- **Human steering.** Patla et al. observed lateral balance adjustments before head and body reorientation. Earlier cues gave subjects more opportunity to adjust foot placement. Use an early shift over the available support, then lead with the head, then rotate the torso and pelvis as travel redirects. [Patla, Adkin and Ballard](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10638436/)
- **Reactive cutting.** Force-plate studies measured braking across the approach and plant contacts, with later cues changing how much could happen before the final plant. A sharper cut depends more on lateral impulse. Author distinct prepare and drive beats, and give a late cue another legal plant when the approach foot is already committed. [Planned and reactive cuts](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10895398/) · [45° and 90° cut mechanics](https://doi.org/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000470)
- **Placement under momentum.** Hof's extrapolated center of mass adds a velocity term to the balance estimate. Gong and Grizzle demonstrated fast turning with contact-relative momentum feedback on Cassie. Use the simpler position-and-velocity estimate to rank candidate feet for the current rigid body. All times, poses, and speed bands below are game tuning targets. [Hof's balance model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17935808/) · [Gong and Grizzle's robot experiments](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10763)

| **Stop or pivot** | A stop loads a forward plant, settles the torso over the feet, then returns to ready. A standing pivot loads the support boot before the other boot travels around it. | Preserve the **90° ±5°** standing pivot target. Keep it a separate action from a moving cut. |

Proposed ground controls use **WASD** for walk, **Alt** for the run modifier, **Ctrl** for crouched guard, **Shift** for brake, **Q/E** for the standing pivot, and **R** for the existing hop. In a forward run, changing from W to D requests a 90° right cut. Adding D while holding W requests a diagonal. A held direction is interpreted in the facing frame captured when the input changed. Hold the resulting world direction through the cut and while the input stays unchanged so chassis yaw cannot keep turning the target. At walking and crouch speed, side inputs can stay as sidesteps.

## Runtime architecture

1. **Authored actions.** Store ground clips in one Godot `AnimationLibrary` with per-foot heel, flat, toe-off and release markers, compatible entry and exit contacts, and a nominal root path. Use the project’s pose-bake pattern and Godot's native track interpolation. Extract the root path into movement and facing requests. Keep joint poses and local pelvis offsets in the clip. At runtime the existing full-body modifier applies the ground sample before the two leg IK passes, sole alignment, and hardpoint capture. Ground samples replace the flight base pose during grounded locomotion. [Existing pose baker](../aerial/poses/bake_pose_library.gd) · [Godot animation tracks](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/classes/class_animation.html) · [Godot root motion](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/tutorials/animation/animation_tree.html)
2. **Steady gait.** Play authored joint motion and foot arcs. Synchronize loop phase to measured travel along the clip's nominal stride, with a bounded rate. Starts, stops, and changes of stance use their own markers so a stationary body can still start the load. Contact markers identify the stance sole for traction and a small terrain correction. On flat ground, the clip supplies the swing trajectory and toe roll. Persistent mismatch exits to a supported recovery instead of letting playback march in place or dragging a foot to catch up.
3. **Transition placement.** At the next usable marker, choose the authored action from current gait, measured approach speed, turn angle, and support leg. Fit at most the next two contacts. Favor the authored placement, a safe lateral balance margin, and enough leverage to brake or push toward the exit. Keep the loaded boot's heading fixed and lock the active heel or toe patch throughout its contact phase. Retarget only a free swing before its commitment marker. Later input can update one pending intent. A failed floor, leg reach, knee clearance, or wall sweep check shortens the step or selects the supported stop.
4. **One physical authority.** Feed the requested planar velocity and yaw to `ground_motion.gd`. Redistribute the existing load budget across the valid feet using the contact weight curves. For a single loaded boot, yaw follows the moment of its actual traction force. With two loaded boots, use the existing opposed force couple for the remaining yaw demand. Clamp each force by available load and friction, and limit the request to the native support footprint's tipping margin. Estimate the impulse from the difference between incoming and requested exit velocity. If the available contacts cannot deliver it within the action, brake sooner, reduce exit speed, or add a gather step. The capsule, pads, and shared actuator solver still supply actual motion. Contact loss immediately drops ground traction and passes momentum and the current pose into the existing airborne logic. [Ground force path](ground_motion.gd) · [Rigid body integration](../aerial/aerial_mech.gd)

The root-path sample and pose phase advance on the physics clock. At a touchdown marker, cache the authored contact patch in terrain space for the lifetime of that plant. Markers describe planned foot loading, and a reachable floor sample plus a native support contact gates actual traction. The camera continues to follow the interpolated rigid body. Hardpoint capture uses the completed skeleton pose.

### A fast right cut, one contact at a time

1. **Accept and prepare.** Capture the rightward vector and measured forward velocity. If the approach foot can still adapt, land it slightly forward to brake and coil toward the available stance. If the cue comes late, keep its contact and use the next legal placement. Begin the load shift before swinging the body around.
2. **Plant and redirect.** Prefer the **left boot** for the outside plant when the current phase allows it. Place it to the left of the predicted center of mass, with some forward offset if there is still approach speed to bleed. A rightward traction impulse then drives the body out of the plant. The right boot opens into the new lane. Preserve the natural entry foot when the preferred plant would require crossing or snapping a loaded boot.
3. **Gather and face.** Once measured velocity has a clear component into the exit, allow chassis yaw to follow the new travel vector. The shoulders can lead after the load cue. Place the trailing boot under the hips along the new lane, keeping the original contact fixed until its toe-off. Exit to a gait at the matching foot phase once heading and travel agree.

For each candidate contact, project the rigid body's center of mass and velocity to the contact time on the support plane. Rank the placement around that predicted position plus the velocity term `v / sqrt(g / h)`, where `h` is effective center-of-mass height above the plane. Bias the outside plant toward the needed brake and lateral impulse, and bound the offset by hip reach, sole clearance, and the traction the support can deliver. This estimate only ranks a few candidates during a transition. The visible pelvis shift communicates load while the native support reactions determine the actual balance.

Start each foot cycle with four poses for catch, weight acceptance, passing, and push-off. Refine the swing and arm timing from those poses. 2. **Connect gait and stance actions.** Add walk and run starts, a loaded stop, crouch entry and rise, and phase-compatible exits. Prove the loop moves at its authored stride speed and keeps the boot in contact on flat ground. Then connect the small terrain correction for the ramp.
3. **Ship one moving cut before expanding.** Author the right prepare, outside plant and gather. Mirror it for the left with explicit bone pairing, keeping armor clearance checks on both sides. Fit early and late cue cases. Reuse those beats for the shallow turn and reversal where their contact roles fit. Keep a distinct standing pivot.
4. **Reconnect hop, catch, skid, and guard.** Blend from the last ground pose and back into a compatible planted phase. Keep the existing impulse, valve, cooldown, traction, and landing checks. Compare the same replay in both views at 30 and 144 FPS.

## Acceptance replay

Start in ready, take two deliberate walk steps, accelerate into the shoulder run, cut 90° right, run out, brake into crouched guard, and take two crouched steps. Replay the cut with both starting support legs, an early and late cue, a mirrored left cut, and a wall-blocked exit. Include a neutral side view when reviewing armor clearance and knee flexion.

- In steady travel, the walk, run, and crouch read as different silhouettes at the same camera settings. Stance feet roll through their marked contact points and the swing legs follow the authored arcs.
- During the cut, a visible load response starts within **0.10 s**. Brake and push occur on valid contacts. The chassis starts turning after lateral travel begins, and the hips follow the new lane without crossing the boots or finishing in a sideways shuffle. At **4 m/s** nominal entry, target completion in **three to four contacts** and **about 2 s**, with travel and facing within **15°** of the exit. The settled pivot retains its separate **5°** error limit.
- Retain **≤0.10 m** planted drift and **≤3°** planted yaw drift outside skids. Log marker time, support leg, actual brake and lateral impulse, travel heading, chassis heading, and ankle error. Assert zero airborne traction, bounded nozzle and contact forces, and no direct transform or velocity writes in normal movement. Use the same physics timeline at both render rates.
